# Video player

<p class="docs-lede">
	A video with its own controls: play, scrub, volume, speed, captions and fullscreen, all reachable from the keyboard,
	over a picture that clears when you stop touching it.
</p>

## Goal

A player for a product tour, a lesson or a recorded talk where the browser's own controls do not match the page. The media is one `video` element; everything around it, the controls, the status and the keyboard, is the recipe's, and none of it needs a player library.

## Primitives

- [MediaController](/docs/controllers/media) owns the playback state: the status, the position, the buffered ranges, whether it is waiting for data, blocked by the autoplay policy or in error, and the volume, the mute and the rate. It also seeks, either at once (`skip`, `seek`) or after the reader has paused on the scrub bar (`scrub`), and it retries a failed load. The recipe never listens to a media event and never stores whether the video plays.
- [CaptionsController](/docs/controllers/captions) owns the captions: it adds the track from the cue data, shows and hides it, and keeps the cues above the controls. The recipe passes the video, the controls element to stay above and one English track, and keeps the button's `aria-pressed` from the state it reports.
- [formatMediaTime](/docs/helpers/media-time) writes the clock, and tolerates the `NaN` duration the element reports before its metadata arrives.
- [cycleValue](/docs/helpers/overview) steps the speed button through 1x, 1.25x, 1.5x, 2x and 0.5x.
- [`rui-slider`](/docs/forms/slider) is the scrub bar and the volume, and the buttons carry the Radiant button classes (`rui-button`, `rui-button--ghost`, `rui-button--square`), so the player follows the selected theme. The component form renders them with the `RuiSlider` and `RuiButton` helpers; the other forms write the slider's light-DOM contract by hand: a root, a track with its fill and one `role="slider"` thumb, with the position seeded in the track's custom properties. The buffered ranges are a `data-buffered` layer inside the scrub track, behind the fill.

Left to the recipe: the keyboard shortcuts, hiding the controls, fullscreen, the states the stage shows (buffering, error with Retry, blocked autoplay), the live region and the accessible names, and loading the clip through a `Blob`.

## Example

Press Play or click the video, drag the scrub bar, change the volume, cycle the speed and turn the captions on. Focus the player and use the keyboard: Space or K plays and pauses, J and L skip 10 seconds, the left and right arrows 5 seconds, the up and down arrows change the volume, M mutes, C toggles the captions, F toggles fullscreen, the digits 0 to 9 jump to that tenth of the video and Home and End go to the ends. While it plays, the controls hide after two and a half seconds without a pointer or key and return on any movement; double-click the video to enter fullscreen. The clip is a twelve second animation made for this page, with a running timecode so a seek is easy to check. It has a sound track of its own. It is played from memory, so the lighter layer behind the thumb, the buffered range, covers the whole clip as soon as it has loaded.

The same behavior as a `RadiantElement`. The markup is authored inside `<video-player-element>` and the element finds its parts with `@query`; there is nothing to mount, clean up or run again.

The element is the plain function's logic on a class. `@query` finds the parts, and `@onEvent` delegates the clicks, the pointer activity and the two sliders' `rui-change` events from the element, so no listener is added or removed by hand and none outlives the element. The keys go through two `createHotkeyController(this, ...)`: one on the stage for the shortcuts, and one on the seek slider in the capture phase, which runs before the slider's own handler and lets it stop the arrow keys the player moves by whole seconds. Any key on the stage also shows the controls, which a delegated `@onEvent` does, because a controller calls back only for a key it matches. `fullscreenchange` is a document-level `@onEvent`. `pointerleave` does not bubble, so its delegated listener runs in the capture phase.

The playback is `createMediaController(this, ...)`, which attaches when the element connects and is destroyed when it leaves, pausing the video, clearing its source and releasing the object URL. Moving the element to another parent destroys and attaches it in the same turn; the controller waits a microtask before it lets go, so the source survives the move, and `onConnected` loads the clip again, which leaves the player paused at the start.

What the plain form writes with `textContent` and `setAttribute`, the element keeps in `@state` fields that `@bindTo` copies onto the markup: the stage's `data-state`, the status and clock text, the speed label, the `aria-pressed` of the four toggles, the error panel and whether the seek bar is enabled. `data-controls-hidden` is a field too, bound to the stage. The hide timer, the `MutationObserver` that keeps the seek thumb's `aria-valuetext`, the position written to the sliders, and the buffered bar stay hand written, because no field follows them. The timer and the thumb observer are stopped by a callback registered once in the constructor with `registerCleanupCallback`, because one registered in `onConnected` would be added again on every move. `createCaptionsController(this, ...)` attaches and is destroyed with the element, and `onConnected` calls `disable()`, because the controller keeps its selection through a move.

</RecipePattern>

The sliders are `RuiSlider` and the buttons `RuiButton`, composed in JSX, so their markup comes from the helpers and the buttons are controlled: `pressed` follows `@state`, and the labels stay fixed. The stage state, the pressed buttons, the speed and whether the seek bar is enabled are `@state` written from `onState`; `@query` finds the `video`, the stage, the controls and the sliders, and the handlers are JSX `on:*` attributes, with the media events left to `createMediaController(this, ...)`. The keys are two hotkey controllers kept in fields: one on the stage and one on the seek slider in the capture phase, because that one has to run before the slider's own handler. Both attach in `onConnected()`, because the stage and the slider are not rendered when the controllers attach at connect.

Nothing that changes every frame goes through `render()`: the clock and the status are bound with `this.$`, and `onProgress` writes the seek position, the volume, the thumb's `aria-valuetext` and the buffered bar to the DOM by hand, so a playing video never redraws the controls. `RuiSlider` has no region for the buffered bar, so `updated()` adds the layer to the seek track and puts it back if a render drops it; the same hook watches the thumb with a `MutationObserver`. The hide timer and the thumb observer are stopped by a callback registered once in the constructor with `registerCleanupCallback`, because one registered in `onConnected` would be added again on every move; `createCaptionsController(this, ...)` and `createMediaController(this, ...)` attach before the first render, when the video does not exist and `getMedia` returns `null`, so `onConnected` calls `captions.refresh()` and `media.attach()` once the video is drawn; `fullscreenchange` is a document-level `@onEvent`, which goes with the element. The fullscreen button is hidden from `document.fullscreenEnabled`, which `onConnected` reads into `@state`, so `render()` never touches the document. `data-controls-hidden` and the fullscreen button's `aria-pressed` are written to the DOM at once and kept in fields that `render()` reads, because a render is deferred and a touch tap or a `fullscreenchange` must show in the same turn.

The playback is destroyed with the element, and moving the element to another parent loads the clip again, paused at the start, from `onConnected`. Compared with the Radiant element above, the markup lives inside the class, so there is nothing to keep in step with the page, but restyling the structure means editing it, and this docs build prerenders the tag empty and the client draws it, so the page shows nothing until the script loads. Mute, speed and play still redraw the stage because `render()` reads those fields; the [Video player store](/docs/recipes/video-player-store) recipe is the same component with the snapshot in one store, so play, mute and speed patch without a render.

The same player as a `RadiantController` attached to the authored markup, which carries `data-controller="video-player"` on a plain `div`.

<RecipeCode recipe="video-player" pattern="controller" />

The class is the plain function's logic on a `RadiantController`: `@query` finds the parts, `@onEvent` delegates the clicks and pointer events on the host, two `createHotkeyController(this, ...)` take the keys (the stage and, in the capture phase, the seek slider), and `@state` fields with `@bindTo` write what is a plain copy of the media state onto the markup (the stage state, the pressed buttons, the clock, the status line, the speed and the error panel), with `connect()` in place of `onConnected()`. The buffered bar and the slider values are still written by hand, because they are not a copy of one value. Nothing differs in what it does. What differs is where it lives: there is no custom element tag to define, so the markup can come from a server template or a CMS and be any element, and the controller attaches to it through `data-controller`. The docs site starts one registry over the document, which connects the controller when the markup appears, including markup inserted later, and disconnects it when the element is removed, so the class only registers itself with `@controller`. `createMediaController` takes the controller as its host as it does an element: it attaches on connect and is destroyed on disconnect, which pauses the video, drops its source and releases the object URL, in a microtask that a move to another parent cancels. The hide timer and the thumb observer are cleaned up on disconnect, and `connect()` loads the clip again, with the captions off (`disable()`), when the element is moved. `createCaptionsController` takes the controller as its host too. `pointerleave` does not bubble, so its delegated listener runs in the capture phase.

<RecipePattern id="plain">

A `mount` function wires the controller to the markup and returns the cleanup that undoes it.

<RecipeExample recipe="video-player" pattern="plain" layout="stack" />

<RecipeCode recipe="video-player" pattern="plain" />

`mount` runs once for each example element on the page, and the function it returns undoes it; see [how the examples run](/docs/recipes/overview#how-the-examples-run). The cleanup destroys the controller, which pauses the video, drops its source and releases the object URL, and it aborts the listeners, clears the hide timer and removes the `fullscreenchange` listener. It leaves fullscreen alone: the element that is full screen leaves the document with the page.

The stage carries the state, `data-state` as `idle`, `buffering`, `playing`, `paused` or `error`, and the stylesheet draws the spinner, the big play button, the error panel and the controls from it; `data-controls-hidden` marks the controls hidden, which only applies while it plays. Both come from pure helpers in `video-player.data`, so every form maps the controller's state the same way. The play button keeps one name, "Play", and says whether it plays with `aria-pressed`, as the mute, captions and fullscreen buttons do; a name that changed with the state would be read twice. The spinner is decoration, and a polite live region says "Playing", "Paused", "Buffering" and "Could not load the video", and nothing at all until the clip has first played (a failure is always said), through `Announcer` in `video-player.data`, so a page that loads is quiet and a moved player does not repeat itself. The speed button keeps its visible text, "1x", in its name ("Speed 1x"), so a voice user can say what they see. The keyboard help is `hidden`, which `aria-describedby` still reads, so it is not read a second time in browse order. The focus ring over the picture is white. The fullscreen button is hidden when `document.fullscreenEnabled` is false, and follows `fullscreenchange` otherwise, so leaving fullscreen with Escape updates it.

The seek slider works in seconds and reports `rui-change` only for the reader, never for the position the player writes to it. Its step of 0.01 s keeps the per-frame position write smooth and is too small for an arrow key, so a capture-phase hotkey controller on the seek slider passes the arrows and the page keys to `skip` before the slider sees them, and a drag goes to `scrub`, which seeks once the pointer has rested. While a scrub is pending the controller reports the target as the position, so the thumb does not jump back. The slider writes its own `aria-valuetext` with every value, so the recipe writes "1:15 of 4:00" after it. The shortcuts are a `HotkeyController` on the stage, built from the `SHORTCUTS` table: a key string with Ctrl, Alt or Meta held never matches, and `isOwnedByControl` declines the activation keys of a button and the keys a slider thumb already handles.

The clip is fetched into a `Blob` by `video-player.loader` and played from memory, passed to the controller as `resolveSource` and `releaseSource`. A media element can only seek in a file whose server answers range requests, and many development and preview servers, this site's among them, do not, so seeking would snap back to the start. For long media, serve the file with range support and give `load()` its URL without a `resolveSource`. Requests for a clip that is still downloading share one download, each with an object URL of its own: the page mounts the next form as soon as the pattern changes, which would otherwise fetch the clip twice. A file that cannot be fetched puts the stage in `error`; Retry calls `retry()`, which loads it again.

### The sample files

The clip and its poster are in `src/public/assets/video` and come from `scripts/generate-video-player-assets.mjs`, which draws the animation on a canvas in Chromium, records it with a generated sound track, and re-encodes the video with a keyframe every second with the ffmpeg that Playwright installs (from `PLAYWRIGHT_BROWSERS_PATH`, or the platform's cache folder). Nothing is downloaded, and running the script again rebuilds the two files. The captions are data in `video-player.data`, passed as the cues of one track to `CaptionsController`, which needs no `.vtt` file. It sets their `line` from the measured top of the controls, so they stay above the bar when it wraps to two rows on a narrow screen; the stage and the video are the same box, so the controls sit inside the video's rectangle.

### Styles

The stage is a 16:9 box with `--shadow-overlay`, and its controls are a gradient bar that fades with `data-controls-hidden`. The buttons keep the Radiant classes and the slider its own parts, and the stylesheet only sets their colors for the dark bar. The `:fullscreen` rule lets the stage fill the screen, and reduced motion turns off the fades and the spinner's rotation. The stylesheet is the `video-player.css` tab in the files above.
